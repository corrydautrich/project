Historical Facts about the School

  • Founded in 1849, among the earliest four-year colleges west of the Mississippi River.
  • Named after William Jewell, who donated $10,000 to help establish the college.
  • Jewell Hall, the first building, was completed in 1858 and remains central to campus.
  • During the Civil War, Jewell Hall was used as a hospital and stables for Union troops.
  • Women were admitted in the early 20th century; fully co-educational by 1920.
  • Many early trustees profited from slavery; enslaved labor contributed to building Jewell Hall.
  • The college acknowledges this history and has a Racial Reconciliation Commission.
  • Baptist-affiliated originally; became independent of Missouri Baptist Convention in 2003.
  • Established the Oxbridge Honors Program in 1982 for international study opportunities.
  • Today, it is a liberal arts college focused on critical thinking, community, and research.
